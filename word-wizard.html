Why bother upgrading a word?

Grammar — in the old, real sense of the word — isn't about worksheets. It's about knowing exactly what a word means and choosing it on purpose. Two people can use the word "good" and mean completely different things. A precise word closes that gap. That's the first skill of the classical trivium: grammar before logic, logic before rhetoric.

Words like said, good, bad, nice, fun, and walked aren't wrong — they're just worn smooth from overuse. Swapping one in doesn't mean your writing has to sound fancy. It means a reader can actually picture, hear, or feel what you meant.

Build the habit now: read the definition, read the example, and ask "does this word say what I actually mean?" That question is worth more than any thesaurus.
